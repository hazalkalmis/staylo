01. The problem

Booking has the inventory.
It doesn't have the decision.

Hotel sites give you 200 results, 40 filters, and three sorting options. You still close 12 tabs and pick the one with the best photo. Staylo replaces all of that with one weighted answer.

Standard hotel search

  • You translate your trip into 20+ checkboxes — and hope you ticked the right ones.
  • For every shortlisted hotel, you open the reviews tab and read for an hour: is it clean, is it safe, is it actually close, what did past guests really say?
  • Sorting by "Top picks" hides the platform's bias behind a friendly default.
  • An 8.4 from 4,000 reviewers and an 8.4 from 38 reviewers look identical — but one is signal and the other is noise. You're left guessing which.

Staylo

  • You describe the trip in your own words. We extract the criteria — quiet, family, walkable, budget — automatically.
  • We read every review for every hotel — across 4 languages — and score the things you asked about: clean, safe, close, quiet.
  • Ranking is weighted by your priorities, not ours. You see the math — and the list rearranges accordingly when you adjust it.
  • One score, blended from Google and Booking reviews and weighted by sample size — so a small-but-loud rating can't hide as a big number.

05. Questions

Things people actually ask.

Is Staylo a hotel booking site?

No. Staylo is a search and ranking layer. We use Booking.com's inventory and prices; you complete the booking with them, at the same rate, with the same cancellation terms.

How do you make money if it's free?

Booking.com pays us a referral fee for completed bookings. That fee comes out of their margin — never from you. You pay the same price you'd pay going direct.

Can the score be gamed?

Hotels can't pay for placement. Reviews come from Booking and a few other public sources; the weighting is yours, not ours. We publish our scoring methodology in full.

What languages are supported?

Search: English and Turkish today, Spanish + French + German next quarter. Review analysis: English, Spanish, French, German — covering ~85% of European review volume.

Why is everything so muted in color?

Because the score color is doing real work — green/amber/red has to mean something. The rest of the UI gets out of the way so the rankings can speak.
